# Kasvanut maisema — "Grown, not drawn"

The scenes in Kvizit are picture-book landscapes of Finland, but nothing natural in them is placed by
hand. Fells, forests, crowns, clouds and auroras are *grown* by small seeded processes. The generator
is the brush: a fixed seed always paints the same picture, and a new seed paints a sibling that looks
just as considered. Every constant in these processes is meant to look like it was tuned over countless
iterations by someone who has spent years watching both northern light and noise functions. The craft
is in the restraint: one more octave, one less ray, the half-pixel that separates *alive* from *busy*.

**Terrain is layered noise under a gentle hand.** A Lapland fell (*tunturi*) is not a triangle. It is a
broad, worn dome, so each ridgeline is a sum of soft cosine-power bumps (round shoulders, flat tops)
carrying a few octaves of fractal value noise for the wear of ten thousand winters. Distance is
expressed through atmospheric perspective: each layer further back is pulled toward the sky colour,
loses detail octaves, and sits lower in contrast. Snow and autumn colour are not stickers placed on a
slope. They are bands computed *from* the ridge, offset down the contour by noise, so they hug the land
the way real *ruska* does.

**Forests are populations, not sawtooth edges.** A treeline is hundreds of individual trees, each with
its own height drawn from a noise field (stands rise and fall together), rasterised into one skyline
and simplified back into a single clean silhouette. Spruces are narrow, tiered and drooping, with a
sawtooth envelope inside a cone. Pines hold their crowns high, and birches are soft. In the foreground the
same grammar scales up: a spruce is a stack of drooping tiers with independent left and right jitter,
and a birch is a noise-wobbled tapering trunk with lenticels that crowd toward the black base. Every
proportion is the product of meticulous observation, not convenience.

**Light is accumulation.** An aurora is a curtain of thousands of vertical rays standing on a folding
baseline. Its sharp lower edge glows green, and its rays fade upward toward violet. Ray heights follow two
noise scales: slow folds and fast striations. The rays are binned by brightness into a handful of paths, so
the curtain is both rich and cheap to render. Stars follow a power law: many faint, few bright, densest
along the Milky Way. Water is the sky folded down, with mirrored silhouettes broken by ripple lines
whose spacing widens with perspective toward the viewer.

**Everything stays paintable.** These scenes are colouring pages for a child. Every generated form
must still resolve into a calm, readable outline: few large organic regions, never a confetti of tiny
ones. Detail lives in non-region texture (grass tufts, bark marks, rays and ripples), which appears only
when the area is painted. Craft here means a master-level balance between density and quiet, so the line
art is an inviting page and the painted result is a living, breathing picture.
